The Light That Looked Down

By The Falconer · 24 October 2026

Before the morning star fell, he came down to look at a man - and saw himself.

Reader, imagine a man - he stands at the edge of the garden as the day retires, the air has no fault in it, the grass beneath his feet has never met a thorn. He has no past to mourn, no grief to live in the shadow of, only the present - and the present is enough for him. Not for long.

Above him there is a light. Not the sun, the sun has gone down - this light does not go down. It moves as a man moves, it walks the ridge of the evening as a general walks the ridge before the armies have assembled. It is the bearer of light, the morning star, the most beautiful of all things God has made - and he has not yet fallen.

He has come down to look at the man.

"So this is him" the light says - not to the man, to himself.

The man looks up. He is not afraid, fear has not yet been born into the world. "Who are you ?"

"I carry the light before the dawn. I was there when the foundations were laid, I sang when the stars were hung in their places. I have come to see what all of heaven is talking about."

"And what is heaven talking about ?"

"You."

Notice: the first wound is not a lie - it is attention.

The man had never been the subject of anything. He named the animals, he tended the garden, he walked with God in the cool of the day - content as a child is content, naive, a shade from the garden before the fall. Now a being of fire tells him heaven speaks his name, and for the first time in all his days the man thinks about the man.

The light sits beside him on the ridge. He does not sit as a servant sits - he sits as a prince sits beside a peasant he has found interesting.

"Tell me what you do" he says.

"I tend what is given to me. I name what is brought to me. I walk with Him."

"And when He is not here ?"

"He is always here."

The light smiles - the most beautiful smile the man has ever seen, he has nothing to compare it to and yet he knows. "Is He ? Then why does He walk with you only in the cool of the day ? Where is He in the heat ?"

The man has no answer, he has never needed one. One thought enters - one thousand march behind it.

*

But Reader, do not think the light is merely the tempter here, look closer. He has not fallen, he does not yet know what he will become. He came down because he was curious, and curiosity is a hunger for the unknown - we desire the unknown, we hate when it is granted.

He looks at the man and sees a thing made of dust that God loves. He looks at himself and sees a thing made of fire that God also loves - but differently ? less ? He cannot tell. He extrapolates, and if I extrapolate all I see, have I really seen anything ?

"Do you know what I am ?" the light asks.

"Beautiful."

"Yes." He says it too quickly - he hears himself say it. A small thing, a trifle, yet the halls of heaven are so quiet a trifle echoes in them for an age.

*

He returns the next night, and the next. Every night the light comes down the ridge, and every night the man waits for it - he waits, hoping one will find him, and one does.

They talk. The light teaches him the names of the stars, the man had only ever called them lights. He teaches him that a thought can be turned over and examined, that you can look at a thing and then look at yourself looking at the thing. Before, the man walked in the garden - now he watches himself walk in the garden. Self consciousness, the first hindrance, the first chain that looks like a bracelet.

"You see" the light says, "you are more than a gardener, you are a mind. The body is temporary, the mind is forever."

The man repeats it in the morning, alone, to the trees. It sounds wise. It sounds like his own.

And the light, flying back up through the fields of heaven, repeats something to himself too - he who once only sang now speaks, and he speaks from high but only a whisper he produces, the wind carries his sound though no one hears it. Nature looks at him, he looks at himself.

*

One night the man asks a question of his own.

"Do you ever wish to be Him ?"

The light goes still. The wind stops. Somewhere in the garden a bird stops in the middle of its song.

"Why would you ask that ?"

"Because you speak of Him the way I have begun to speak of you."

And here, Reader, is the true moment - not the serpent, not the fruit, not the war of swords and fire. The moment is a question from a creature of dust, and the light looks into it as into a well polished mirror, and sees what he has been carrying the whole time without naming it.

He came to look at the man. He has been looking at himself.

"I was the first light" he says slowly. "Before the sun, before you. I was the one who held it up so the rest could see - was it not me ? Who carried it ? Who stood closest ? And He takes dirt and breathes into it and gives it a garden and walks with it - and I am to bow ?"

The man says nothing. He recognises the voice. It is his own voice, from the mornings, speaking to the trees.

"Adequacy" the light says, "is the enemy of those who strive for greatness."

"Is it ?" the man asks.

"Is it not ?"

And neither of them knows anymore who taught whom.

*

"I will tell you a secret" the light says, "and you will keep it for me. There is nothing you cannot do - nothing. He tells you there are limits because limits keep you small. Pride is only the name the small give to the great."

The original lie - you can do whatever you want. Notice: he did not tell it to the man first. He told it to himself, the man was merely in the room.

The man lies awake that night. He holds one thing in his left hand, another in his right. In the left the garden, the cool of the day, the walking, the being known. In the right the light, the mind, the stars with their names, the feeling of being spoken of in heaven. He knows he can only hold one at a time. Tears are in his eyes - he does not yet know what tears are for.

*

What happened after is written elsewhere, by other hands - the war, the third of the stars swept down, the lightning falling from the heights. Reader, those are the drums of war, and the battle cries are only the foreshadowing of the screams and clanks of Hades. I am not interested in the war. I am interested in the night before it.

The night before, the light came down the ridge one last time. He did not walk like a general now. He walked like a man - and that is the most terrible thing I can tell you.

"I am going to ask Him" he said. "Tomorrow. I will stand before the throne and ask Him why."

"And if He answers ?"

"Then I will have my answer."

"And if He does not ?"

The light looked out over the garden, so beautiful, without fault - and for a moment the man saw something pass over the face of the most beautiful thing ever made. Not anger. Grief. The grief of one who already knows what he is about to destroy, and will destroy it anyway, because he has convinced himself he is infallible - because he has already made up his mind.

"Then I will build my own heaven" he said. "Out of whatever is left."

The man wanted to ask him - can one sleep in hell ? But he did not. He had learned, from the light, that some questions are better kept.

*

In the morning the sky was the same. That is what no one tells you - the sky was the same. The sun rose, the garden breathed, the animals carried their names. But the ridge was empty, and it stayed empty.

The man waited. He waited, hoping one would find him - no one came.

What he did not understand, what he would not understand until the fruit was in his mouth and the cool of the day became a thing to hide from, is that the light had not left. It had only stopped being outside of him.

It sat now in the halls of his mind, conversing among his thoughts. It said: you have been awake long enough, rest. It said: only you could think these things. It said: maybe if you sin in private He won't see. It was never loud. Gossip is never explicit - words of war are whispered esoterically.

*

Reader, you were told the devil fell because he wanted to be God. Perhaps. I think he fell because he looked down at something beneath him and saw himself in it, and could not bear what he saw - so he called it greatness and walked into the fire, telling himself it was the light.

He subjected himself to hell willingly; he only told himself it was glory he was walking into.

And the man ? The man did what men do. He built a fortress of his mind and called it wisdom, he retreated into it like an army routing from battle, and he sealed the cave shut. He lived in the shadow of a grief he could not name - grief for a friend who was never his friend, for a light that was only ever a mirror.

Every night he looked up at the stars. He knew all of their names now. He searched for the brightest one, the one that rose before the dawn.

It was never there.

What force could redeem such a man - surely not himself.
