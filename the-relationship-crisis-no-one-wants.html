The Relationship Crisis No One Wants to Talk About

By The Falconer · 29 October 2026

From the perspective of someone under 25, modern relationships increasingly feel transactional, shallow, and insecure. Dating no longer seems rooted in trust, shared purpose, or long-term commitment, but instead in appearance, convenience, and social validation. Relationships are often less about genuine connection and more about how they look online — who you’re seen with, what you post together, and how desirable that makes you appear to others.

Social media and dating apps have fundamentally changed the dynamic. At any moment, your partner may have dozens of alternative options sitting in their pocket. This constant availability of replacements undermines security and discourages emotional investment. Why commit deeply when everything feels provisional?

Many young men feel particularly frustrated by the modern dating landscape. Third-wave feminism, in their view, has promoted high expectations from men while rejecting traditional reciprocal roles such as cooperation, support, or complementarity. Instead, career advancement and individualism are prioritised, often leaving relationships feeling one-sided or conditional.

This has created a distorted dating market, especially among Generation Z. A small group of highly successful, wealthy, or high-status men tend to attract a disproportionate share of female attention, while a large number of other men are left excluded entirely. These men are often labelled “incels,” but in reality many are simply disengaged, discouraged, or opting out altogether. Interestingly, even the men who succeed most in this system often report dissatisfaction. They describe partners as over-demanding, emotionally volatile, and shaped by extensive prior relationships that make genuine bonding difficult.

At the same time, there is a growing sense of fear among men. Dating now carries not only emotional risk, but reputational and legal anxiety. The idea that a single accusation — true or false — could permanently damage one’s life has made many men cautious, withdrawn, and hesitant to pursue relationships at all.

Women, however, are far from happy in this environment either.

Many express frustration that men appear weak, passive, or unwilling to initiate. Masculinity feels diluted. Men often lack direction, confidence, or a clear path in life. This is not simply a personal failure, but a generational one. Many young men grew up without strong father figures and were shaped more by online culture than by real-world male role models. The result is uncertainty about how to act, lead, or commit.

What emerges is a mutual resentment. Men feel rejected, disposable, and undervalued. Women feel disappointed, unsupported, and unimpressed. Each side blames the other, while the underlying structures that created this situation go largely unexamined.

The result is a genuine relationship crisis.

The divide between young men and women has reached an all-time high — socially, politically, and economically. Trust is eroding. Shared expectations are disappearing. Intimacy is increasingly commodified, and long-term commitment feels risky rather than rewarding.

This is not simply a cultural mood swing. It is a systemic breakdown driven by technology, economic pressure, ideological confusion, and the collapse of shared pathways into adulthood. Yet it remains one of the least openly discussed crises of our time.

Until society confronts these issues honestly — without reflexive blame or ideological defensiveness — the alienation between the sexes will continue to deepen. And the longer it goes unaddressed, the harder it will be to repair.
