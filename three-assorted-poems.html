Three Assorted Poems

By The Falconer · 29 October 2026

Reader, imagine a man—his mind a fortress, his heart a captive to the passions he so desperately attempts to govern. He sits alone, pondering the past, the present, and that which may never come to pass.

The Archangel Gabriel appears before him, bestowing upon the man three poems. He looks upon them, recognising in each a part of himself—one of love departed, another of love restrained, and the last of love imagined.

Could this be a gift of consolation, or merely another trial of the passions? Has providence granted him wisdom, or has the man been given another means to enslave himself to his own delusions?

He cannot know. Alas, what he once sought to command has now become his master. He takes up his pen, for the words of the mind are more comfortable than the words of the physical.

I. Her

Years after your passing, melancholy reigns tyrannically.

Somber he who is alive; appeased is she who is no more.

In the world you left behind, the living envy the dead.

My will to love you is here; your will to resurrect is not.

Defiled in life you were; in death, sanctified you are.

Your piano still plays, each note a reminder.

Your smile still shines, each memory an indicator.

Long nights with you, longer eternity without you.

II. Under This Sun

Thinking about you, under this sun—however hot and dusty it is.

The rays beam down onto me, beating me dark.

However, what beats me more is you not being here—sitting at this table alone—your smile and warmth, vacant.

However, such is life. As the sun retires each day, my mind does not retire from you, for without need for sword nor stick, you appear to have captured my mind.

Rendered me a hostage, a prisoner of my own delusions—I do not dare to cast the dice, to play with fortune, to ask you back into my life.

For I know my obligation: keep you at arm’s length, for I know honour comes before dismay—dismay at my delusions being just what they are.

III. Your Smile Still Glows

Your smile still glows, teeth white—lips plump, and your beautiful skin, caramel—just like mine—a delight for only the most knowledgeable.

For you are rare, one not found so easily. I know losing you would be to lose more—to lose hope, perhaps.

Could it be you have become an idol, a person who I made an image of—just that, an image, something still and never a showing of the present reality?

My stoicism persists, not allowing you to attain me—though not physically, you shall not attain my mind.

The mind wanders on, wishing you thought about it too—we all know the reality.

I can’t help but concoct your smile—I cannot help but to concoct a plan, a plan to win you over. Oh, how the old general was silenced by the sword.

Poetry, my only way to communicate with you.

Look at the stars, I ask… where is mine?

The man lays down his pen, pondering the message bestowed upon him by Gabriel. Is this consolation from the heavens, or merely another affliction of the earthly passions? The angel has departed, yet the words remain—each a reminder of that which he possesses no longer, that which he desires, and that which may never be.

Could providence have sent consolation in the form of suffering? Or has the man, in his perpetual pursuit of meaning, mistaken the chains of his own desires for gifts from the heavens?

Alas, the messenger has spoken, yet the man remains without an answer. Is it Gabriel’s message he fails to understand, or is it himself?
