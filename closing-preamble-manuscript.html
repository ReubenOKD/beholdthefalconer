Closing Preamble: Manuscript

By The Falconer · 5 November 2026

I finish reading.

The philosopher stands beside the window, his eyes directed towards the world outside.

He turns.

“Have you found what you sought?”

“No.”

“Then what have you found?”

I consider the question.

A man who desires, yet wishes to appear indifferent. A man who proclaims the rule of reason, yet permits his passions to determine which objects reason must investigate.

A man who wants to be approached, yet retreats from the very person whose approach he desires.

Alas, what a pitiful contradiction.

The philosopher approaches the desk.

“Do you still believe the figure possesses some hidden meaning?”

“Perhaps.”

“And what if it does not?”

“Then I have wasted my time.”

“And if it does?”

“Then I have failed to discover it.”

“Must these be the only possibilities?”

I remember the warnings of my own conscience - do not fall into false dichotomies.

Yet here I am, having constructed another.

I tell him, “There was once a time when the woman smiled upon seeing me, when words passed between us without difficulty, when her presence was an ordinary part of my day. Now I find myself avoiding her eyes, measuring my conduct, wondering whether to speak or remain silent. Surely something has changed?”

“Something has changed,” he replies.

“Then you acknowledge it?”

“Yes.”

“What has changed?”

“Tell me.”

I become irritated.

“Must I provide every answer myself? You were among the greatest philosophers of your civilisation, yet you stand before me asking questions which I have spent weeks attempting to answer!”

He remains silent.

Then he asks, “Have you considered that the change which troubles you may not exist entirely within her?”

I look away.

The heart becomes agitated.

It desires to defend itself, to bring forth its evidence, to establish once and for all that the events were not imagined.

Yet the mind begins to consider another possibility.

What if I have altered the very conditions by which I observe her?

What if my attempts to conceal my desires have made me appear indifferent?

What if the apparent distance between us is not entirely a distance she has created?

Alas, the man who hides his thoughts from another then laments that they remain unknown.

But what if she truly is indifferent?

Then let her be indifferent.

What authority have I over the will of another? I may govern my own conduct, my words and the direction of my actions, yet I cannot command the thoughts of a woman who possesses a life entirely separate from mine.

The philosopher looks towards the triangle.

“Why do you require this figure to be completed?”

“Because I desire to know what it represents.”

“And if no conclusion can be reached?”

“Then I shall remain uncertain.”

“Would uncertainty destroy you?”

I consider this.

Perhaps not.

Yet what strange torment it is, to possess a mind which demands finality while living in a world which offers little of it.

The philosopher takes the manuscript.

“Young man, you have devoted yourself to the investigation of a woman, yet I suspect you are searching for something beyond her.”

“What might that be?”

“Ask yourself.”

I look towards the books upon my desk.

The philosopher who had spent his life examining the nature of knowledge now asks me to examine the very instrument with which I seek it.

How strange.

I tell him, “Perhaps I merely desire the restoration of what once existed.”

“And what existed?”

I hesitate.

A smile? Conversation? Companionship? The possibility of something greater?

Or perhaps merely the hope that another human being might recognise something within me which I have struggled to recognise within myself.

The philosopher remains silent.

I ask, “Is it not possible that providence brought us together?”

“It is possible.”

“Then perhaps there is some purpose to it?”

“Perhaps.”

“And if I never discover that purpose?”

“Would it cease to have existed?”

I cannot answer.

The philosopher approaches the doorway.

“Remember,” he says, “the world does not suspend its movements while one man attempts to understand them.”

With those words he departs.

I am alone.

The room returns to silence, the books remain in their former positions, the triangle lies before me.

Had he truly appeared?

Or had the mind, fatigued by its own reflections, constructed another world in which to continue its arguments?

Perhaps I have not spoken to the philosopher at all.

Perhaps I have merely allowed my own conscience to speak through the image of a man whose writings I hold in such high regard.

Alas, how cunning is the mind!

Even in the pursuit of truth it may create an illusion, then rebuke itself through the very illusion it has created.

I look towards the triangle once more.

What is the third point?

Is it the relationship between the two persons? Their positions within the physical world? The meaning attached to their movements?

Or is it the observer, the man who stands beyond the figure, imagining himself detached from the very thing which consumes him?

I cannot say.

I know what occurred before my eyes.

I know what I remember.

But what have I truly established?

The woman remains beyond my knowledge, living within her own world. Perhaps she remembers these events, perhaps they are trifles to her, perhaps what I regard as extraordinary has been entirely ordinary in her experience.

I cannot enter her conscience.

Yet I have spent so much time attempting to do so that I have neglected the government of my own.

What a strange reversal - the philosopher who sought to understand the world has become a slave to his interpretation of it.

I rise from the desk.

The lamp burns low.

I look towards the darkness outside.

Tomorrow the world will continue. The woman may smile, she may not. She may speak, she may remain silent. She may return to her former place, or perhaps that place has ceased to hold any significance to her.

I shall have to live regardless.

But have I truly accepted this?

Or have I merely written another argument in favour of a virtue which I have not yet acquired?

I extinguish the lamp.

The triangle remains.

And as I depart the chamber, one final question presents itself:

Have I been investigating the distance between myself and another, or has the greatest distance all along been that between the man I claim to be and the man who conducts himself before her?

Alas, perhaps the third point was never hers to provide.
